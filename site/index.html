The phone isn't the price.
Getting to it is.

Availability trackers tell you where an iPhone is in stock. They don't tell you what it costs to be standing in that shop. This ranks every Apple market by landed cost — the device in local currency, minus any tax refund you'd actually recover, plus the round-trip airfare from your airport.

It exists because of one specific problem: the US doesn't sell an iPhone 18 Pro with a physical SIM tray. Neither do Canada, Japan, or the UAE. If you need one, you have to leave — and the cheapest phone is rarely the cheapest trip.

Why the cheapest phone loses

The cheapest phone on the board is rarely the cheapest trip. A market can undercut everyone on the device and still lose once the airfare lands on top — while a pricier one wins because it is close. That inversion is the entire point, and no availability tracker can show it.

Airport stores count double

Some Apple Stores sit inside the terminal — Jewel Changi is one. For those routes a layover can be enough: you never clear immigration, never book a hotel, never lose a day. Stores inside an airport are marked ✈ above.

No invented savings

Tax refund rates are real money and vary by scheme, operator and minimum spend. Where the scheme isn't verified against a source, this counts the refund as zero rather than guessing. Two are settled: Hong Kong has no VAT at all, and the UK abolished visitor refunds in 2021.
